## Introduction: the tool that got you here

If you run a small business, there is a good chance a spreadsheet is doing at least some of the work. Orders in one sheet, expenses in another, maybe a customer list somewhere else. And there is nothing wrong with that — a spreadsheet is cheap, fast, and everyone knows how to use it.

The question is not whether spreadsheets are bad. The question is when they stop being enough. And the answer is not a product or a price. It is a set of signs that show up in the daily routine.

This article lists those signs. Not as a sales pitch for any tool — as a checklist you can apply to your own work this week.

## Sign 1: the same data is typed twice

Someone takes an order on the phone, writes it in a chat, then types it into a spreadsheet. Or a customer's name is entered in the booking sheet and again in the invoice sheet. Every duplicate entry is a chance to make a mistake and a waste of a minute.

Ask yourself: how many times does the same number or name get entered by a human before it reaches its final form? If the answer is more than once, the system is leaking time.

## Sign 2: history is buried in chats

"Where did that order go?" "Scroll up in the group." When your business history lives in message threads, finding an old record becomes archaeology. A spreadsheet does not fix this by itself — but when the spreadsheet and the chats both hold parts of the truth, neither is complete.

The real question: if you wanted last month's numbers today, how long would it take you to assemble them?

## Sign 3: reports arrive late

If the weekly or monthly report is always a day or two late — because assembling it is a separate job that no one has time for — the spreadsheet is not reporting, it is a filing cabinet. Reporting should be a click, not a project.

## Sign 4: everything depends on one person

If only one person knows how the sheet works — which column feeds which, where the formula broke last time, what the colours mean — the business has a single point of failure. Holidays, sick days and resignations turn into data disasters.

A system that more than one person can use is not a luxury. It is continuity.

## Sign 5: the same mistakes keep repeating

A wrong price that keeps appearing because the master list is not centralised. A customer charged twice because two sheets disagree. When errors repeat, they stop being accidents and become a structural problem — the data model, not the people.

## What the next step realistically looks like

Here is the good news: the next step is not "buy an expensive ERP system". For a small business, it usually looks like one of these:

- **A shared database or panel** where the data lives in one place and everyone sees the same thing.
- **A small automation** that moves data between tools so nobody types it twice.
- **A reporting view** that shows today's numbers without assembly.

The scale matters less than the direction: one source of truth, fewer manual steps, reports that arrive on time.

## Before you change anything

Take one week and note every time you re-enter data, search for a lost record, or wait for a report. Those notes are your real requirement document — more useful than any sales brochure.

## Conclusion: spreadsheets are a beginning, not a ceiling

A spreadsheet that got your business this far has done its job. The signs above are not failures; they are growth. The next step does not have to be big. It has to be one step.
