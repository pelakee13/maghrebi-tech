## Introduction: the appointment book, in the middle of doing everything else

When I started building a salon management system with Heli Beauty Studio, appointments lived in a paper notebook and coordination lived in scattered messages — sometimes a phone call, sometimes a chat, sometimes a note in the corner of the book. None of these tools are bad; the problem is that together they do not make one picture. At the end of the day, assembling "who, when, which service" became one more job on top of the job.

This case study explains what was built for this salon, what changed and — most importantly — which salons this system is not a good fit for.

## At a glance

- **Client:** Heli Beauty Studio (beauty salon)
- **Product:** custom salon management system — Persian-language web app
- **My role:** full design and development of the web app, from idea to delivery
- **Status:** the project is complete and has been delivered to the client.
- **Key result:** the paper notebook and scattered messages were replaced by one shared panel the whole salon uses every day.

## The challenge: several tools, one scattered picture

Before the system, salon information was spread across several places: appointments in the notebook, coordination in messages, customer details in various places. This created three specific problems:

- **Duplicate work:** every coordination meant a phone call or a message, and then a manual note.
- **Errors:** an appointment lost between messages or written down wrong in the book.
- **No shared picture:** everyone saw the situation from their own angle; there was no single view of the working day.

## The solution: a Persian panel for the salon's daily work

Instead of off-the-shelf software with extra menus, I built a custom web app that gathers Heli's daily work in one simple Persian-language panel: customers, appointments and daily operations — the things the salon deals with every day, and nothing more.

The design criterion was simple: if the salon's staff cannot use the panel without long training, the panel was not built for them. So a Persian interface, uncluttered pages and a focus on the salon's real workflow sat at the centre of the design.

## The result: a shared panel used every day

Today at Heli Beauty Studio, the paper notebook and scattered messages have been replaced by one shared panel — a panel the whole salon works with every day. When appointments and customers live in one place, coordination turns from "several calls and messages" into "one look at the panel".

## Which salons this system is not right for

Honestly: for a salon that does not yet have a steady flow of customers, or where everything runs well with one simple notebook, this system is not a necessary purchase. If you only have a few appointments a day, the notebook is enough. This system is worth it for salons where the volume of appointments and coordination has grown past "memory and notebook" and needs a shared picture.

## Conclusion

A salon management system does not have to be complicated; it has to give everyone a shared picture of appointments and customers that the whole salon uses every day. Heli Beauty Studio's experience was exactly that: a simple Persian panel replaced the notebook and scattered messages.
